## New this run — California Civil Code § 1675, liquidated damages (for the earnest money reel)

Read verbatim at Justia, 2025 California Code:
<https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-2/title-4-5/chapter-2/section-1675/>
(Justia labels this the 2025 Code and notes it "may not be the most recent version." History note on
the page: "Repealed (in Sec. 1) and added by Stats. 2008, Ch. 665, Sec. 2. Effective January 1, 2009.
Section operative July 1, 2014, by its own provisions." No later amendment shown.)

- **(a) Scope**, verbatim: "'residential property' means real property primarily consisting of a
  dwelling that meets both of the following requirements: (1) The dwelling contains not more than
  four residential units. (2) At the time the contract to purchase and sell the property is made,
  the buyer intends to occupy the dwelling or one of its units as his or her residence."
- **(b)** A liquidated damages provision is valid "to the extent that payment in the form of cash or
  check, including a postdated check, is actually made if the provision satisfies the requirements of
  Sections 1677 and 1678 and either subdivision (c) or (d)."
- **(c) At or under 3%**, verbatim: "If the amount actually paid pursuant to the liquidated damages
  provision does not exceed 3 percent of the purchase price, the provision is valid to the extent
  that payment is actually made unless **the buyer establishes that the amount is unreasonable** as
  liquidated damages."
- **(d) Over 3%**, verbatim: "If the amount actually paid pursuant to the liquidated damages
  provision exceeds 3 percent of the purchase price, the provision is **invalid unless the party
  seeking to uphold the provision establishes that the amount actually paid is reasonable** as
  liquidated damages."

Limits on how this may be used in content:
- The 3% figure is about **liquidated damages actually paid**, not a cap on what deposit a buyer may
  offer. Do not write "your deposit is capped at 3%."
- §1675 only shifts **who has to prove reasonableness**. It does not say the seller automatically
  keeps 3%, and it does not decide any individual dispute. Content says "general information, not
  legal advice" and points people to their contract and their own counsel.
- (b) ties validity to §§1677 and 1678 as well (separate signing/initialing formalities). Content
  does not enumerate those — it says the clause has its own signing requirements.
- **The CFPB earnest money page could not be read this run**, so nothing in the reel is sourced to
  CFPB. Everything legal in it traces to §1675 as quoted above.

## Worked investor math (for the cap rate vs cash-on-cash post)

My own arithmetic, stated in content as an example with its assumptions on the graphic and in the
caption — **not a quote, not a projection, not a specific property.**

Assumptions: $900,000 two-unit property; $2,700/month per unit ($64,800/yr gross); 5% vacancy;
$22,000/yr operating expenses (taxes, insurance, maintenance, water/trash, reserves); 25% down
($225,000) plus about $15,000 closing costs; $675,000 loan, 30-year fixed, principal and interest
only at 7.32%.

| Line | Figure |
|---|---|
| Effective gross income (after 5% vacancy) | $61,560 |
| Less operating expenses | −$22,000 |
| **NOI** | **$39,560** |
| **Cap rate** (NOI ÷ price, no loan) | **4.40%** |
| Monthly P&I on $675,000 at 7.32% | $4,636.78 |
| Annual debt service | $55,641 |
| Annual cash flow (NOI − debt service) | **−$16,081** (about −$1,340/mo) |
| Cash invested | $240,000 |
| **Cash-on-cash** | **−6.70%** |
| Break-even rent to cover opex + debt service | about **$3,405 per unit/month** |

- The teaching point: **cap rate ignores the loan, cash-on-cash does not.** A 4.4% cap looks
  unremarkable; the same deal financed at today's average is negative $1,340 a month.
- **Rate caveat that must appear in the content:** 7.32% is Freddie Mac's survey average for
  owner-occupied 30-year fixed loans. **Investment-property financing typically prices above that**,
  so the real cash-on-cash on a rental would usually be worse, not better. The post says to get an
  actual quote rather than using the survey average.
- Rounded for the graphic: cap "4.4%", cash-on-cash "−6.7%", cash flow "about −$1,340 a month".
