## California resale market — C.A.R. September 2026 report
Source: California Association of REALTORS®, released Oct 19, 2026
(<https://www.car.org/aboutus/mediacenter/newsreleases/2026releases/sept2026sales>)
Figures below were confirmed verbatim in the C.A.R. release text as carried by Yahoo Finance and
the Mountain Democrat (the car.org page itself truncated before the release body).

Statewide, existing single-family homes:
- Sales: **260,410 SAAR** in Sept 2026 — down 3.4% from 269,620 in Aug 2026, down **6.5% YoY**
  from 278,540 in Sept 2025. Below 300,000 SAAR for the 48th straight month.
- Year-to-date sales up 0.8% through September.
- Median price: **$899,720** — down 0.2% from $901,420 in Aug 2026, up **1.7% YoY** from $884,390.
- Median price per square foot: **$434**, up 1.4% MoM and **1.6% YoY**.
- Unsold Inventory Index: **3.7 months**, unchanged from August, vs 3.6 months in Sept 2025.
- Median days on market: **29 days**, down from **32 days** in Sept 2025.
- Sales-price-to-list-price ratio: **98.8%** (Sept 2026) vs **98.2%** (Sept 2025).
- Active listings: up 2.7% MoM, up only **0.5% YoY**.
- Share of sales $1M+: 35.9%, up from 35.2% in August.
- C.A.R. cites a 6.86% average 30-year rate for September (6.67% Aug, 6.35% a year earlier).
  This is a monthly average and sits below the late-October PMMS weekly reading — do not mix the two.

Regional (median price Sept 2026 / Aug 2026 / Sept 2025):
- **Southern California**: $895,000 / $900,000 / $869,250. Price -0.6% MoM, **+3.0% YoY**.
  Sales -0.2% MoM, **-9.1% YoY** — the largest annual sales decline of any California region.
- **Los Angeles Metro Area**: $855,000 / $850,000 / $830,000. Price +0.6% MoM, +3.0% YoY.
  Sales +1.5% MoM, -8.0% YoY.
- County-level LA and Orange County figures appeared only in a non-verbatim extract of the
  release table and are NOT used in content until confirmed.

Quotes (verbatim):
- Tamara Suminski, C.A.R. President: "As we move into fall, the market feels a little less rushed,
  giving buyers more time to carefully consider their options."
- Jordan Levine, C.A.R. SVP & Chief Economist: "On the supply side, the mortgage rate lock-in effect
  continues to constrain new listings, while limited inventory is helping to cushion home prices
  against weaker demand."
